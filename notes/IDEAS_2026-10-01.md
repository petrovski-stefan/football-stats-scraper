    code builds any disallowed path.
  - **The disallowed paths are mostly data feeds** (`ajax`, `flashLive`, `textlive`). Our rule
    already says never call them directly, so the direct-call part matches the site's wish.
  - **The open question got bigger.** The pages' own JavaScript may load these feeds while
    our browser renders them; the live page's goal and event timelines may arrive that way.
    Two readings:
    1. robots.txt covers the URLs a bot chooses to open; a page's own background loads are
       part of rendering it, like for a human visitor. Then we comply today.
    2. A strict bot fetches no disallowed URL at all, page resources included (Googlebot
       works this way). Then the live and detail pages cannot be rendered with their data,
       and those scrapes stop.

    - **Strict render** (those paths blocked in the browser): the h2h page never renders

    - **Reading:** under the strict reading, the detail and live scrapes are impossible. A
      rule that blocks jQuery also makes the page unusable for any visitor that renders it,

### 16 — Formatting, rounding, duplicate columns

- **Confirmed on the EPL file:** `ht_score` is `1-0`, `ft_score` is `4 - 2` in the same row.
  Cause: `scrapers/parsers.py` stores the page's text as found, and the two scores come from
  different DOM spots. The history blocks in `_detail.csv` do the same.
- **Proposed fix:** drop the score strings. The integer columns (`ht_home_goals` and the
  others) already hold the same facts.
- **Rounding:** derived floats are stored at full precision (`implied_prob_home` =
  `0.7692307692307692`). Proposed rule:
  - never round in the data files: rounding drifts sums (implied probabilities stop adding to
    the overround);
  - round only in reports: 3 decimals for probabilities, 2 for odds.
  - After the refactor most of these floats leave `data/raw/` anyway (they are derived).
- **Duplicate candidates to check:**
  - the score strings vs the integer columns (exact duplicates);
  - schedule `home_win_initial_odds` vs detail `odds1x2_init_home`, and `implied_prob_home`
    vs `implied_init_home`. Two pages, maybe two bookmakers: compare the values before
    dropping either;
  - columns derivable from others: `total_goals`, `goals_1h`, `goals_2h`, `is_comeback`
    (= home OR away). These move to the derived files in the refactor.
- **Output:** a short naming and format rule list, applied in the refactor.

### 19 — Deeper integrity checks

- **Today, `qa/league.py` checks only that a position lies in 1..team count.** No check
  rebuilds the table.
- **Table check (first):**
  1. Rebuild points and goal difference round by round from our own results.
  2. Compare with the scraped positions. Points are not scraped today.
  3. Without per-league tie-break rules, test only the order: a team must never sit above a
     team with more computed points. A violation is a scrape error or a points deduction.
- **Expected mismatches that are not bugs:** tie-break rules (head-to-head vs goal
  difference), points deductions, postponed matches played out of round order, split and
  play-off stages.
- **Other checks, using data we already hold twice:**
  1. Goal store vs scores: goals per side, and goals before 45', equal FT and HT scores.
  2. History rows vs schedule rows: a match seen in a later fixture's history block has the
     same score and odds as its own schedule row.
  3. Season completeness: n teams → n(n−1) matches in a double round-robin.
  4. Calendar: no team plays twice on one day.
  5. Events: substitutions per side within the league's limit; minutes within range.
- **Decided 2026-10-02: keep the per-round standings scrape** on every league-season. It is
  the answer key for the table check, every round. Idea 10 does not drop it. The cost (one
  dropdown click and wait per round) is not measured; idea 10 can measure it.

### 20 — User-agent rotation

- **Rotation already exists:** `scrapers/driver.py` picks one of 7 strings in
  `scrapers/constants.py` for each driver.
- **The list is the problem, not the lack of a library:**
  - all 7 are from late 2022 (Chrome 108/109);
  - 2 claim Safari, 1 Linux, 2 Mac, but the real browser is a current Chrome on Windows;
  - the browser's other signals (client hints, `navigator.platform`) still say Chrome on
    Windows, so the mix is easy to spot;
  - a Safari string can make the site serve a different layout, which breaks parsers.
- **A library (e.g. fake-useragent)** gives newer strings but keeps the same mismatch.
- **Options, decided in 10 together with the ethics question:**
  1. one fixed string that matches the installed Chrome (simplest, consistent);
  2. rotate only among current Chrome-on-Windows strings.
