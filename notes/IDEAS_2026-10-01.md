### 5 — Market explainers

- One doc per market, sorting every fact into three kinds:
  - **definitional** — true by the settlement rule (no comeback with fewer than 3 goals), so
    never computed;
  - **structural** — follows from the goal model, so derived once, in the doc;
  - **empirical** — needs data; only these get measured and preregistered.
- Written like GLOSSARY: concepts and math, invented example numbers, no project results.
- **The user's choices for the comebacks explainer (2026-10-04, asked before writing):**
  in-depth teaching with every fact tagged; only the bets the label settles (2/1, 1/2), plus
  one card separating them from "win from behind"; the event and its price (implied
  probability, margin, break-even), no EV, CLV or staking; mechanisms only as open
  questions; formulas with one worked example, no derivations; ASD-STE100; self-contained,
  repeating GLOSSARY's definitions; empirical questions with no pointers to results.
  Ask again before the next explainer.
- **Format added after two outside reviews the user ran (2026-10-04):** every structural card
  has a Basis line: proof (true for every setting of the model), grid check (true for the
  stated settings only) or example. Calculations about tests (sample sizes, counts by
  chance) are labelled statistical design, not structural. Every number is checked by a
  second, independent calculation before it goes in. The summary table gives each claim its
  own row with exactly one basis (proof, grid check, example, or assumption for the model
  itself); a grid-check row starts "In the checked settings", and an example row names the
  example.

### 16 — Formatting, rounding, duplicate columns

- **Confirmed on the EPL file:** `ht_score` is `1-0`, `ft_score` is `4 - 2` in the same row.
  Cause: `scrapers/parsers.py` stores the page's text as found, and the two scores come from
  different DOM spots. The history blocks in `_detail.csv` do the same.
- **Proposed fix:** drop the score strings. The integer columns (`ht_home_goals` and the
  others) already hold the same facts.
- **Rounding:** derived floats are stored at full precision (`implied_prob_home` =
  `0.7692307692307692`). Proposed rule:
  - never round in the data files: rounding drifts sums (implied probabilities stop adding to
    the overround);
  - round only in reports: 3 decimals for probabilities, 2 for odds.
  - After the refactor most of these floats leave `data/raw/` anyway (they are derived).
- **Duplicate candidates to check:**
  - the score strings vs the integer columns (exact duplicates);
  - schedule `home_win_initial_odds` vs detail `odds1x2_init_home`, and `implied_prob_home`
    vs `implied_init_home`. Two pages, maybe two bookmakers: compare the values before
    dropping either;
  - columns derivable from others: `total_goals`, `goals_1h`, `goals_2h`, `is_comeback`
    (= home OR away). These move to the derived files in the refactor.
- **Output:** a short naming and format rule list, applied in the refactor.
